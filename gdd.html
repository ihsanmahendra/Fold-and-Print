Fold & Print

A paper-folding puzzle. Each level is a sheet with wet paint blots and a target picture. Pick where to fold: paint prints onto the other side as a mirror image, and where two colors land on each other they mix.

1. Core mechanic

Screen layout: target card at the top ("Make this", fold counter, color recipe), the paper sheet in the middle, crease tabs along the bottom (vertical folds) and right edge (horizontal folds), FOLD button below.

Core loop

  1. Compare the sheet with the target.
  2. Tap a crease tab. A dashed line shows the crease; dotted rings preview where paint will land, and a red X marks a smudge.
  3. Tap FOLD. The paper flips over, presses and opens: both sides print onto each other.
  4. Repeat until the sheet matches the target. The print is framed in your Gallery.

Fold rules

Mirror printEvery cell is mirrored across the crease; both sides print onto each other.
MixingTwo colors on one cell mix into a new color.
Paper edgeCells folded past the edge print nothing, so off-center creases protect one side.
PermanentPaint can't be removed. Only adding is possible.

Objective: reproduce the target picture with a fixed number of folds.
Win: the sheet matches the target exactly.
Lose: paint lands where the target has none ("Smudged!"), or every fold is used without a match. Fail offer: Undo last fold (rewarded ad) or start over.

Why it is satisfying

  • The reveal. Unfolding a sheet into a perfect symmetric print is a natural "wow", the same pleasure as the ink-blot butterfly from art class.
  • Mixing payoff. Blue and yellow pressing into green is surprising and colorful every time.
  • Few moves, big thinking. Most sheets take 1 to 4 folds, so each choice matters and a win feels earned.
  • Tactile animation: flip, press, peel. It suits haptics and ASMR sound design.

What is different from the market

  • Folding is not new; printing is. Existing paper-fold puzzles fold colored paper to complete a picture. Here folding transfers wet paint, so one fold changes both sides at once.
  • Additive, permanent mixing. A wrong fold leaves a visible smudge, so every mistake is readable.
  • Give/receive mechanics. Tape, dry paint, stamps and water drops change which cells print or accept paint.
  • A fresh verb in today's charts. The leaders (jam, sort, screw and conveyor games) move or clear pieces; Fold & Print transforms the whole board in one action.

2. Gameplay depth

Depth grows on three dials: how many folds (1 to 5), which creases (center, off-center, diagonal) and what the paper does (tape, dry paint, cut-outs). Each world turns one dial, so the game always feels like one new idea.

LevelsWorldNew ideaWhat the player learns
1–10BugsOne center fold, single colorsFold = mirror print
11–25GardenMixing: green, orange, purplePlan which color meets which
26–40SeasideOff-center creases, 2 foldsThe paper edge protects one side; order
41–60WorkshopTape, dry paintShielding and sequencing
61–80FestivalDiagonal folds, 3 foldsNew symmetry axis
81–100Night skyStamps, cut-outs, double-thick paperPartial prints, holes
101+RemixMechanics combined, 4–5 folds, larger sheetsMastery

How difficulty rises but stays intuitive

  • The rule never changes. Every mechanic only edits which cells give or receive paint.
  • Preview before commit. Difficulty comes from planning ahead, never from hidden rules.
  • More folds, more combinations. A 7×7 sheet has 12 creases, so 3 folds give about 1,700 sequences; players reason backward from the target.
  • Sawtooth pacing: every 5th level Hard, every 10th Super Hard with bonus coins, then 2 easy levels.

Why it lasts 100+ levels

  • Levels come from a target picture plus a solution; a solver checks each one. The prototype already brute-forces every fold sequence per level.
  • The printed picture is the content and the reward, so each level looks new.
  • From level 100, recombining 6–8 mechanics on larger sheets gives near-endless variety.

Boosters

  • Undo: take back the last fold; the main fail-screen offer.
  • Hint: shows the next correct crease.
  • Eraser: removes one smudged cell.

4. Design paradigm

Why the mechanic is fun

  • Anticipation, then reveal. Every fold has a beat of suspense and a payoff.
  • Low input, high thinking. One tap changes the whole board.
  • Mistakes teach. A smudge shows exactly where paint went and why.
  • Real-world intuition. Everyone already understands folding paper and mixing paint.

Meta layer: prints are framed in a Gallery the player decorates room by room with coins.

How it avoids repetition

  • One new mechanic per world: a can't-fail intro, a practice level, then combinations.
  • Every level is a new picture.
  • Level types rotate: one-fold "spot the crease", multi-fold planning, tape-order puzzles, big poster sheets.
  • Events every 2 weeks refresh goals without new code.

5. Marketability

Why players would download it

  • The ad shows the childhood ink-blot fold and asks "where would you fold?"
  • The answer is visible in one second, ideal for fail ads and "choose the right fold" ads.
  • Every win produces a pretty, shareable print.

Ad angles to test

  1. "Which fold?" Three crease options; wrong pick smudges, then the right one.
  2. ASMR print: slow-motion close-up of paint pressing and peeling apart.
  3. Impossible picture: a complex poster from 4 folds, "only 3% can print this".

What makes it stand out

Fold puzzles exist, but they fold paper to complete a shape. Fold & Print folds to print: wet paint transfers both ways, colors mix, and a wrong fold smudges. Every fold is a setup, a choice and a reveal in under 5 seconds.

Why it can perform now

  • Proven hybrid casual model: rewarded Undo on fail, interstitials after level 10, booster IAP, ad-free pack.
  • Cheap to produce: 2D art, one core interaction, levels built from pictures with an auto-solver.
  • Easy creative iteration: a single fold is a complete story in under 5 seconds.

Prototype and 4-week production plan

The playable prototype has 6 sheets covering mirror printing, mixing, off-center creases, tape and dry paint, with fold preview, smudge fail and Undo. Every sheet was brute-force checked: each has a winning sequence, and most first folds smudge.

Soft-launch scope for a team of 1 programmer, 1 artist and 1 game designer: 60 levels, mechanics 1–4, boosters and the Gallery.
